# Idea 1: Missed-order check

**Pitch.** Every morning, know that every order your shop took yesterday reached your automation, and get the list of the ones that did not.

**Gap.** When a webhook never arrives, the workflow never runs, so there is no error to catch. Shopify also removes a webhook subscription after repeated failed deliveries, and from then on nothing runs. Delivery tools such as Hookdeck sit in the webhook path. The fix people describe is outside that path: compare the shop's own order list with the log of what was processed.

**Who has the pain.** A freelancer or two-to-ten person agency that built order automations in n8n, Make or Zapier for Shopify stores, at the moment a store owner forwards a customer asking where their order is.

**Proof.**
1. "None of these show up as a failed execution, so you only notice when a customer asks about their order." TrenoxaHQ, 2026-10-23. https://community.n8n.io/t/323887/1
2. "If a webhook never reaches the workflow, there’s no failed execution for the error handler to catch." Yana1, 2026-10-23, on the same problem in Make. https://community.n8n.io/t/323887/2
3. "The row appears in the Requests table, and then nothing happens. The owner is on a job, the office phone is ringing, and the new row sits unread until evening." Huzaifa, Glide community, 2026-10-28. https://community.glideapps.com/t/87820/1

**Pain score: 20/25**

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 3 | One 44-post thread plus the same failure on Make and Glide; not hundreds of people |
| Intensity | 4 | Lost orders and customers chasing |
| Willingness to pay | 3 | They build workarounds and hire freelancers; no one quoted a price |
| Reachability | 5 | n8n and Make forums, Shopify partner directory |
| Buildability | 5 | Two API reads and a comparison |

**Closest competitors and why they miss.**
- [Hookdeck](https://hookdeck.com/blog/recovery-surge-webhook-failure-mode.md): webhook queueing and retries for developers. Its own advice is to run a separate reconciliation job; it does not ship one for shop owners.
- [MESA](https://getmesa.com/blog/backup-workflow-shopify-demo): a Shopify automation app that teaches the reconciliation pattern inside its own workflows. It does not check n8n, Make or Zapier.
- [Mechanic](https://github.com/lightward/mechanic-docs/blob/main/core/shopify/events/reconciling-missing-events.md): documents reconciling missed events, for its own tasks only.
- A freelancer on [Contra](https://contra.com/s/beFFYZP3-duplicate-and-missing-order-reconciliation) sells this as a one-off service, which is evidence that people pay.

**Price guess (estimate).** $29 per store per month, or $99 per month for an agency with up to five stores. Reasoning: one missed order usually costs more than $29, and agencies can pass it on as a maintenance line.

**MVP.**
- Core: connect a store (read-only orders), choose where processed orders are logged (Google Sheet, Airtable or a webhook the workflow calls), run a daily comparison, and email or Slack the missed orders. Also alert when the webhook subscription has disappeared.
- Skip: replaying orders, other shop platforms, dashboards, team accounts.
- Stack: Lovable for the front end, Supabase for the database and scheduled functions, Shopify Admin API, Resend for email, Stripe for payment.

| 6-9 | Hosted version: Shopify connection, sheet connection, daily job, alert email |
| 10-11 | Subscription-missing alert; Stripe checkout |
| 12-14 | Onboard three stores; ask each for payment |

**First 10 customers.** The posters in the n8n thread; people answering the n8n and Make "looking for contractors" posts; Shopify automation freelancers on Contra and Upwork.

Message: "I read your post about Shopify orders that never reach the workflow. I'm building a daily check that lists any order your automation missed. Can I run it on one of your stores for a week, free, and you tell me if it found anything?"

**Biggest risk and cheapest test.** People may treat it as a free template, since the thread already explains how to build it. Test this week: offer the hosted check to ten automation freelancers at $29 per store and count how many give a card.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?
- Fewer than two of ten freelancers will pay, because copying the forum workflow is good enough.
- n8n, Make or Shopify ships a "missed events" check. Shopify could do this in a release.
- The original poster turns out to be selling the same thing. The handle reads like a business name; I did not confirm it.
- Missed orders prove rare in practice, so the alert never fires and people cancel.

# Idea 2: Approval receipt

**Pitch.** A single approval link for automations and AI agents that records which named person approved which exact action, in a form an accountant accepts.

**Gap.** Workflows pause for a click in Slack or Telegram. The click proves someone pressed a button. It does not prove who, and it is not tied to the exact payee and amount that went out. Finance products such as Bill.com and Ramp have real approvals, but only for payments made inside them.

**Who has the pain.** A freelancer or small agency that built an invoice or refund automation for a client, at the moment the client's accountant asks who approved a payment.

**Proof.**
1. "has a client, their accountant or their bank ever asked who approved a payment? what did you show them?" viv2004, n8n community, 2026-11-06. https://community.n8n.io/t/327105/1
2. "there is no field on that boundary for who answered, and no principal is modelled there, so any code holding the thread can resume it, including the process that raised the pause." TroyClifford, LangChain forum, 2026-10-28. https://forum.langchain.com/t/4966/1
3. "I wouldn’t let it pay on its own for food or supply vendors, even small amounts from known suppliers. The invoice is often not what actually showed up." Leslie_Fleming, an executive chef, n8n community, 2026-11-06. https://community.n8n.io/t/327105/3

**Pain score: 18/25**

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 3 | Three forums in 30 days, small threads |
| Intensity | 4 | Money leaves the account; audit questions |
| Willingness to pay | 3 | Nobody named a price; they build sheets and logs |
| Reachability | 4 | Same forums as idea 1 |
| Buildability | 4 | Sign-in, a signed record and an export |

**Closest competitors and why they miss.**
- n8n "send and wait" and Zapier human-in-the-loop steps: pause and resume, with no verified identity and no record tied to the payload. I did not open their documentation tonight; check before building.
- HumanLayer and gotoHuman: approval layers for developers building agents. My search returned nothing on either, so their current features are unverified.
- [hoop.dev](https://hoop.dev/blog/human-in-the-loop-approval-for-tool-using-agents/) and Gravitee: enterprise gateways with audit logs, priced and built for security teams.
- Bill.com, Ramp: approvals only for payments inside their product.

**Price guess (estimate).** $39 per workspace per month including 200 approvals. Reasoning: cheaper than an hour of an accountant's time, and it sits next to automation tools that cost $20 to $60 per month.

**MVP.**
- Core: one HTTP call creates an approval request with the exact payload; the approver signs in by email link; approve or reject; the workflow gets the answer; a record stores who, when, and a hash of the payload; export to PDF and CSV.
- Skip: rules engines, amount limits, mobile app, single sign-on.
- Stack: Lovable, Supabase (auth, database, row-level security), Resend, Stripe.

| Days | Work |
|---|---|
| 1-3 | Ask ten automation builders what they showed the last time someone asked who approved a payment |
| 4-8 | Request endpoint, sign-in, approve page, callback |
| 9-10 | Record and export |
| 11-12 | n8n and Make example workflows |
| 13-14 | Two pilot users on real invoices |

**First 10 customers.** Repliers in the two threads above; n8n and Make freelancers who advertise invoice automations; bookkeepers who use Make.

Message: "You wrote about invoice workflows that wait for an approval in Slack. If the client's accountant asked who approved a payment, what would you show them today? I'm testing a small tool that produces that record."

**Biggest risk and cheapest test.** The question in quote 1 was asked by someone who looks to be researching the same product, so the pain is asked about more than it is complained about. Test this week: ask the ten builders the question above. If fewer than three have ever been asked for proof, drop it.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?
- Fewer than three of ten builders have ever been asked who approved something.
- n8n or Make adds signed-in approvals with an export. This is a feature a platform can ship quickly.
- HumanLayer or gotoHuman already serve no-code users well. I could not check.
- Accountants reject a third-party record and want it inside the accounting system.

# Idea 3: Host rota for recurring groups

**Pitch.** Keeps a weekly or monthly in-person group going by rotating who organises, confirming headcount two days ahead, and telling the host when to cancel or invite more.

**Gap.** Meetup, Luma, Partiful and Heylo manage an event page and RSVPs. Search found nothing that spreads the organising work across the group, which is what people say kills groups. This is the part of the market that AI does not replace: the product is a room with the same people in it every week.

**Who has the pain.** The one person who organises a recurring board-game night, run club, supper club or circle of 6 to 20 people, about a year in, when they are tired and the group stops if they stop.

**Proof (all from January 2026, outside the 30-day window).**
1. "No-one else in the group ever organised anything. It got really tiring." marcus_holmes, Hacker News, 2026-01-16. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46641707
2. "trying to arrange people to play board games is quite difficult because you can't predict how many people will show up." SchemaLoad, Hacker News, 2026-01-15. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46640438
3. "You have to be the one who reaches out. You have to do it on a regular cadence." neilk, Hacker News, 2026-01-15, 41 replies. https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46640332

All three are from one thread with 799 points and 1,245 comments: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46635345

**Pain score: 19/25**

| Criterion | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 5 | Top-voted advice in a 1,245-comment thread, repeated by many |
| Intensity | 3 | Burnout and groups ending; no money lost |
| Willingness to pay | 3 | Organisers pay Meetup and Heylo; friend groups pay nothing |
| Reachability | 4 | Meetup organisers are listed publicly by city |
| Buildability | 4 | Rota, reminders and a headcount poll over SMS or WhatsApp |

**Closest competitors and why they miss.**
- Meetup: listing and RSVPs; organiser fee reported at $45/month (unverified).
- [Heylo](https://heylo.com/pricing): member management and dues for lasting groups, $19 to $199/month. May have co-leader features; I did not confirm.
- Partiful, Luma: single-event invitations.
- Howbout: shared calendar for friends.
- Pie ($24M raised): matches strangers into groups of six and is reported to be moving toward permanent communities. This is the largest threat.

**Price guess (estimate).** $12 per group per month, paid by the organiser. Reasoning: below Heylo's $19 tier and well below the reported Meetup fee.

**MVP.**
- Core: a group, a schedule, a rota of hosts, a message two days ahead asking "in or out", and a summary to that week's host.
- Skip: discovery of new members, payments, an app, chat.
- Stack: Lovable, Supabase, Twilio or WhatsApp Business messages, Stripe.

| Days | Work |
|---|---|
| 1-3 | Message 20 Meetup organisers of small recurring groups; five calls |
| 4-7 | Run the rota and headcount by hand for two groups using a spreadsheet and text messages |
| 8-12 | Build group, rota and reminder flow |
| 13-14 | Move the two groups onto it; ask for $12 |

**First 10 customers.** Organisers of board-game, running and supper-club groups on Meetup in Toronto with 6 to 30 regular attendees.

Message: "I see you've run this group for a while. Who organises when you can't? I'm testing a small tool that rotates the host and confirms numbers two days before. Could I run it for your next four sessions for free?"

**Biggest risk and cheapest test.** The proof is ten months old and comes from one technical forum. Test this week: 20 messages to organisers. If fewer than five reply and none say the organising load is their main problem, drop it.

### WHAT WOULD KILL THIS IDEA?
- Organisers say the real problem is finding members, not sharing the work.
- Heylo already has rotating hosts and headcount confirmation.
- Members ignore the "in or out" message, so headcount stays unpredictable.
- Nobody pays $12 because a WhatsApp poll is free.
- Pie's move to permanent communities covers it with $24M behind it.
